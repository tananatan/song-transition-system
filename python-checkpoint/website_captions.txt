Page title: Song Analysis & Transition

Data used
This preliminary analysis uses two real tracks by Broke For Free: Something Elated and Night Owl. We examined Something Elated from 3:28 to 3:52 and Night Owl from 0:00 to 0:24. These windows were chosen manually as an ending-to-beginning transition candidate. The plots use original song timestamps and unmodified audio, averaged to mono for analysis.

1. Waveforms and RMS level
We plotted each waveform and calculated RMS in 50 ms frames. RMS gives a simple measure of signal level. Something Elated is stronger through most of its selected window, then drops sharply near 3:49. Night Owl starts more quietly and builds toward the end of its window. This helps identify where a level adjustment or crossfade might be needed. RMS is not a perceptual loudness measurement, and this plot alone cannot show which transition sounds best.

2. Estimated rhythmic activity
We calculated spectral flux, which measures increases in spectral magnitude between short time frames. The markers show detected sound attacks, not confirmed beats. We then used autocorrelation to look for repeating pulse rates. Something Elated has strong candidates near 87.6 and 178.2 BPM, showing approximately half/double-time ambiguity. Night Owl has candidates near 129.2, 86.1 and 64.6 BPM. The correlation values are not confidence probabilities. We still need to verify the musical beat by listening before using these estimates for alignment or time stretching.

3. Frequency content over time
We used the short-time Fourier transform to show how frequency content changes within each window. Brighter colors show larger spectral magnitudes, using the same color scale for both songs. Night Owl's opening is concentrated at low frequencies, while Something Elated contains stronger content across the displayed range before its late drop. This helps explain why bass overlap is worth checking during the crossfade. The plot shows 0-4000 Hz only, and does not prove that a bass filter improves the transition.

Current status
The code produces three real-data analysis figures. Window selection is still manual. Automatic selection, verified beat alignment, and pitch-preserving time stretching are not implemented in this script. The next step is to compare a plain crossfade with the filtered preview by listening, then decide which analysis features are useful.
