

1. Waveform
The line shows positive and negative audio samples. A narrower band suggests lower signal level; a wider band suggests more activity. Look for an ending that becomes less busy rather than a sudden cutoff. Low amplitude alone does not prove a good transition.

2. RMS signal level
The line summarizes the level every 50 ms. Look for a smooth reduction in the outgoing song and room for the incoming song to enter without a sudden level jump. Listen to check musical phrasing and avoid cutting off a vocal or melody. RMS is not perceptual loudness.

3. Frequency content
Time goes left to right, frequency goes bottom to top, and brighter colors mean stronger frequency content. Look for whether both songs have strong low-frequency material at the same time; overlapping bass can compete. This plot does not automatically identify instruments or choose a good mix.

The windows are Something Elated 208-232 s (3:28-3:52) and Night Owl 0-24 s. These were manually chosen. All analysis uses the average of the two stereo channels, which can reduce sounds with opposite phase. Original stereo files are unchanged.
